# Magnus's notebook: rules from my earlier games

1. [middlegame] Play every capture chain on a square to the end, recounting attackers and defenders after each trade; a piece never defends the square it moves to Example: ...Qc3 "forked" rooks but bxc3 won the queen; 27...Rf5 was "backed" by the rook that had just moved
2. [middlegame] Before any quiet or developing move, list every enemy check and every capture on your loose pieces; if one wins material, pick another move Example: 11.Bc4?? Qh4+ 12.Bf2 Qxc4 with f3 played and king uncastled, 55%→8%
3. [middlegame] After castling on the opposite wing, spend a tempo on Kb1/Kh1 prophylaxis before starting operations, instead of releasing central tension Example: Caro-Kann: 13.Ne4 traded at once; Stockfish preferred 13.Kb1 then Rh4 with a lasting attack
4. [middlegame] When you have more space or an attack, avoid trades that ease the defence; instead improve your worst-placed piece and keep pieces aimed at the enemy king Example: 13.Ne4 and 15.Qe2 swapped attackers off; Kb1, Rh4 and Ne5 build-ups scored better
5. [middlegame] Before trading a minor piece, play the recapture: if a pawn recapture opens a file/diagonal toward your king or leaves pawns loose, keep the piece Example: 17...Nc4 18.Bxc4 dxc4 opened the e-file for Re4 and left c4 hanging, 51%→9%
6. [middlegame] When a wing pawn is fixed (h5 vs h6), lift the rook to the 4th/5th rank (Rh4) and swing it to the other side instead of waiting Example: Caro-Kann after 8.h5 h6: Rh4 was the engine's main idea in several positions, not more piece trades
7. [middlegame] Each move, name your undefended pieces and the entry squares (c2, d3, g4, your 6th/7th rank) an enemy unit can reach; guard the loosest first Example: Loose bishop plus loose pawn gave Black free tempi ...Nb4, ...d5, ...f5, then a rook invaded d6-c6
8. [middlegame] Keep a knight or bishop on an advanced square only if a pawn can support it; retreat before enemy pawns remove every escape square Example: Unsupported Ne4 had no safe retreat after e5/d6; Bd5 and Be4 were chased by pawns
9. [middlegame] Never push pawns in front of your own castled king while an enemy bishop or queen eyes those squares; answer a wing storm with a central break Example: 17.g3 invited ...fxg3 and ...Bxh3; 17.dxc6 kept a winning game
10. [middlegame] When a bishop pins your knight against king or loose rook, kick the pinner with a3/h3 or Bd2 rather than defending passively with the queen Example: 12.Qd2 let ...Nc5-e4 pile on; 12.a3 questioned Bb4 at once, 59%→54%
11. [middlegame] When a rook can invade the enemy second rank hitting loose pawns, prefer that to any equal trade; with exposed kings, activity beats simplification Example: Instead of 17...Nc4, 17...Rf2 grabbing h2 and tying White down was winning
12. [opening] Delay the central break (d5/e5) until minors are out and the king is castled; when two units can recapture, use the minor piece, not the queen Example: 8...d5 exd5 left a backward pawn, 48%→42%; 10...Qxd5? 11.Qxd5 lost a tempo, 45%→38%
13. [endgame] Before trading into a pure pawn endgame, count both kings' distance to every pawn and the race in moves; equal material loses if his king is faster Example: 28...Rxe5 29.Rxe5 looked drawable 4v4, but White's central king outpaced mine, 50%→6%
14. [endgame] With extra material, trade one-for-one but never your last extra piece; first cut off his king and take the 7th, push a pawn only with escort Example: Refusing 31.Rd5 dropped 94%→61%; later 59...b5 pushed an unescorted pawn instead of Rd5
15. [endgame] Keep extra pawns connected, stay inside the square of every passer, and before each move check enemy Kx-captures and stalemate traps Example: 66...Qc3+? Kxc3 drew a won queen endgame; 29...dxc4 scattered a6/c4/c6 into losable pawns
