# Magnus's notebook: rules from my earlier games

1. [middlegame] Before any move, list enemy checks, captures and pawn-captures hitting your destination square; never place a piece where a pawn can take it for less value Example: 35...Bd5?? 36.exd5 swapped my extra bishop for a pawn, 51%→8%
2. [middlegame] When material up, trace every enemy knight two moves ahead for forks (Nc4-b6 hitting a8/c8); kill the entry square or step the attacked rook away, not the bishop Example: 24...Nf6? and 26...Bb7? allowed Nb6xa8; 24...Ba4/26...Rcb8 kept the piece
3. [middlegame] Play each capture chain to the end, recounting attackers and defenders after every trade; a piece never defends the square it moves to Example: ...Qc3 "forked" rooks but bxc3 won the queen; 27...Rf5 was defended by the rook that moved
4. [endgame] With two minor pieces versus a rook, refuse every piece-for-pawn trade; blockade the passer with the knight, keep the bishop on a pawn-free diagonal, march the king in Example: 35...Ne6 held; 35...Bd5 36.exd5 collapsed to rook versus knight
5. [middlegame] Keep a piece on an advanced square only if a pawn can support it; retreat it before enemy pawns remove every escape square Example: Unsupported Ne4 had no retreat after e5/d6; Bd5 and Be4 were chased by pawns
6. [endgame] In rook endings, before every rook move ask if an enemy rook can land on your back rank; with king behind untouched f2-g2-h2, play h3/g3 or Kf1 first, or meet an invading rook with Rd1/Rd2 opposition Example: 29.Rd4?? Rc1+ 30.Rd1 Rxd1 mate, 56%→2%
7. [endgame] When your rook can capture an enemy rook with check, take it; fear of a pawn race never justifies declining a capture with check while material up Example: 28.Rd3+ instead of 28.Rxc7+ threw away 82%→54%
8. [endgame] With extra material trade one-for-one but never your last extra piece; cut off his king, take the seventh rank, push a passer only with escort Example: Refusing 31.Rd5 dropped 94%→61%; 59...b5 pushed an unescorted pawn
9. [endgame] Before each endgame move check enemy Kx-captures and stalemate tricks, keep extra pawns connected, and stay inside the square of every enemy passer Example: 66...Qc3+? Kxc3 drew a won queen endgame; 29...dxc4 scattered a6/c4/c6
10. [middlegame] Before trading a minor piece, play out the recapture: if a pawn recapture opens a file or diagonal toward your king or leaves pawns loose, keep the piece Example: 17...Nc4 18.Bxc4 dxc4 opened the e-file for Re4 and left c4 hanging, 51%→9%
11. [middlegame] When one of your pieces is attacked, first look for a pawn move that counterattacks or gains tempo, and test whether a check removes the retreat square Example: 25...Nd6? lost to 26.Qg5+ Kh7 27.Qxf6; 25...c5 held
12. [middlegame] When your rook can invade the enemy second rank hitting loose pawns, prefer that to any equal trade; with exposed kings activity beats simplification Example: Instead of 17...Nc4, 17...Rf2 grabbing h2 and tying White down was winning
13. [middlegame] Never push pawns in front of your castled king while an enemy bishop or queen eyes those squares; answer a wing storm with a central break Example: 17.g3 invited ...fxg3 and ...Bxh3; 17.dxc6 kept a winning game
14. [opening] Delay the central break (d5/e5) until minors are developed and the king is castled; when two units can recapture, use the minor piece, not the queen Example: 8...d5 exd5 left a backward pawn; 10...Qxd5? 11.Qxd5 lost a tempo
15. [opening] When a bishop pins your knight against king or a loose piece, kick the pinner with a3/h3 or block with Bd2 instead of defending passively with the queen Example: 12.Qd2 let ...Nc5-e4 pile on; 12.a3 questioned Bb4 at once
