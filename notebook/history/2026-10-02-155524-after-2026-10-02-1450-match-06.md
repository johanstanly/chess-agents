# Magnus's notebook: rules from my earlier games

1. [middlegame] Before any quiet or developing move, scan every enemy check; if a check (Qh4+/Qa5+ when f3 is played or the king sits uncastled) wins your piece, choose differently Example: 11.Bc4?? Qh4+ 12.Bf2 Qxc4: f3 plus uncastled king made the e1-h4 check decisive, 55%→8%
2. [middlegame] Count every unit that can capture your destination square and play out the whole recapture sequence; move only if the final material count favours you Example: ...Qc3 forked rooks but bxc3 won the queen; 26.Ne5 Rxe5 Nxe5 Rxe5 gave two knights for a rook
3. [endgame] Never place a piece on a square the enemy king attacks unless your own piece guards it; test Kx and stalemate first, even when winning easily Example: 66...Qc3+? Kxc3 drew a won Q endgame; later 58...Rd4 let 60.Kxd4 take my rook
4. [endgame] With overwhelming material, improve king and rook first (cut the enemy king off, seventh rank) and advance pawns only when king or rook escorts them Example: 59...b5 pushed an unescorted pawn instead of Rd5; loose pawns and a stray rook let the king counterattack
5. [endgame] When material ahead, welcome equal trades and simplify: rook plus pawns beats bishop plus pawns; only bare rook versus bishop is drawn Example: Refusing 31.Rd5 trade "to keep the exchange" dropped winning chances 94%→61%
6. [opening] Delay the central break (...d5/...e5) until your minor pieces are out and your king is castled; otherwise the opened lines favour the developed side Example: 8...d5 exd5 with Nb8 and king home left a backward e5 pawn and open e-file, 48%→42%
7. [opening] When two pieces can recapture, prefer the minor piece; recapturing with the queen onto a square the enemy queen eyes just hands them the trade for free Example: 10...Qxd5? 11.Qxd5 Bxd5 reached the same structure a tempo down with queens gone, 45%→38%
8. [middlegame] Each move name your undefended pieces and the entry squares (c2, d3, g4, your sixth/seventh rank) an enemy piece can reach; guard the loosest before acting Example: Loose bishop plus loose pawn gave Black free tempi ...Nb4, ...d5, ...f5; the rook then invaded d6-c6
9. [middlegame] Keep a knight or bishop on an advanced central square only if a pawn can support it; otherwise retreat before pawns chase it into having no squares Example: Unsupported Ne4 had no safe retreat after e5/d6; Bd5 and Be4 were chased by pawns
10. [middlegame] Never push pawns in front of your king while an enemy bishop or queen eyes those squares; answer a wing pawn storm with a central break or file-opening capture Example: 17.g3 invited fxg3 and Bxh3; 17.dxc6 kept a winning game
11. [endgame] Keep extra pawns connected and mutually defended; choose the recapture that keeps the chain linked even if it doubles a pawn Example: 29...dxc4 scattered a6/c4/c6 and Rd6-b6-a6 ate them; bxc4 held everything
12. [endgame] Before your king or rook grabs material, count the pawn race and confirm your king stays inside the square of every enemy passer Example: 44...Kxh4 won a rook but f4-f5-f6 plus the h-pawn made two queens
13. [opening] When your bishop is attacked, pick the retreat that checks or gains a tempo and prevents the opponent's planned central push, not the passive square Example: 6...Bb6 instead of Bb4+ allowed e5 and the d6 wedge; chances fell 50%→32%
14. [opening] When an enemy pawn wedges into your camp on the sixth or seventh rank, capture or blockade it immediately; trades that leave it alive cost material Example: 10...Nxd2 ignored the d6 pawn; d6-d7xe7 then wrecked my structure
15. [opening] Before an exchange sequence on an open file, recount the defenders of your central pawns after every single trade, not just the first Example: 8.a4 axb5 axb5 Rxa8 removed e4's guard and lost the pawn to ...Nxe4
