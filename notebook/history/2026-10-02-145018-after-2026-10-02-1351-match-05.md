# Magnus's notebook: rules from my earlier games

1. [middlegame] Before any quiet move, scan every enemy check; if a check (Qh4+/Qa5+ when f3 is played or king uncastled) wins the piece you just placed, choose differently Example: 11.Bc4?? Qh4+ 12.Bf2 Qxc4: f3/uncastled king made the e1-h4 check decisive, 55%→8%
2. [middlegame] List every enemy unit that can capture your destination square — pawn, piece, king — and play the move only if the recapture count favours you Example: ...Qc3 forked rooks but bxc3 took the queen; Ng5 died simply to hxg5
3. [middlegame] Count the entire capture sequence on a contested square; if stacked heavy pieces already hit your piece, trade or retreat it — adding a cheap defender feeds the sequence Example: 26.Ne5 and 27.Nhf3: Rxe5 Nxe5 Rxe5 handed over two knights for one rook
4. [middlegame] When material ahead and a piece of yours is attacked, prefer offering an equal trade over a retreat that keeps tension; simplify toward a clearly won ending Example: 31.Rc3 (saving the rook actively) instead of 31.Rd5 trading into won R-vs-B: 94%→61%
5. [endgame] Rook plus pawns versus bishop plus pawns is a win — trade the last rooks happily; only bare rook versus bishop with no pawns is drawn Example: Refusing the rook trade "to keep the exchange" halved my winning chances in R+R vs R+B
6. [endgame] With extra material prefer the active move — rook to the seventh, attack weak pawns — over slow luft moves; play Kh2/h3 only against a concrete back-rank check Example: 32.Kh2 instead of 32.Rc7 gave Black time for ...g6 and a defensive setup
7. [middlegame] Each move name your undefended pieces and the squares (c2, d3, g4, your sixth/seventh rank) an enemy piece can enter; guard the loosest before starting anything active Example: Loose bishop plus loose pawn gave Black free tempi ...Nb4, ...d5, ...f5; rook then invaded d6-c6
8. [middlegame] Keep a knight or bishop on an advanced central square only if a pawn can support it; otherwise retreat before pawns chase it and it has no squares Example: Unsupported Ne4 had no safe retreat after e5/d6; Bd5 and Be4 were chased by pawns
9. [middlegame] Never push pawns in front of your king while an enemy bishop or queen eyes those squares; meet a wing pawn storm with a central break or file-opening capture Example: 17.g3 invited fxg3 and Bxh3; 17.dxc6 kept a winning game
10. [opening] When your bishop is attacked, pick the retreat that checks or gains a tempo and stops the opponent's planned central push, not the passive square Example: 6...Bb6 instead of Bb4+ allowed e5 and the d6 wedge; chances fell 50%→32%
11. [opening] When an enemy pawn wedges into your camp on the sixth or seventh rank, capture or blockade it at once; trades that leave it alive cost a piece Example: 10...Nxd2 ignored the d6 pawn; d6-d7xe7 then wrecked my structure
12. [opening] Before an exchange sequence on an open file, recount the defenders of your central pawns after every single trade, not just the first Example: 8.a4 axb5 axb5 Rxa8 removed e4's guard and lost the pawn to ...Nxe4
13. [endgame] Keep extra pawns connected and mutually defended; choose the recapture that keeps the chain linked even if it doubles a pawn Example: 29...dxc4 scattered a6/c4/c6 and Rd6-b6-a6 ate them; bxc4 held everything
14. [endgame] Before your king grabs material, count the pawn race and confirm your king stays inside the square of every enemy passer Example: 44...Kxh4 won a rook but f4-f5-f6 plus the h-pawn made two queens
15. [endgame] With queen versus lone king, never place the queen on a square touching that king unless your own king guards it; test Kxq and stalemate first Example: 66...Qc3+? Kxc3 threw away a won K+Q vs K and drew
