# Magnus's notebook: rules from my earlier games

1. [middlegame] Count the full capture sequence on a square, not just defender numbers: if two enemy rooks hit a knight defended by a knight, you lose two pieces for one rook Example: 26.Ne5 with black rooks on e7/e8: Rxe5 Nxe5 Rxe5 won two knights for a rook
2. [middlegame] If your piece is already hit by stacked heavy pieces, trade it off or retreat it at once; adding a cheap defender only feeds the exchange sequence Example: 27.Nhf3 "defended" Ne5; 27.Nxd7 was needed, instead Rxe5 Nxe5 Rxe5 collected both knights
3. [middlegame] Do not push g3/h3 or move pawns in front of your king while an enemy bishop eyes h3/g2; keep the shield and break in the centre instead Example: 17.g3 invited fxg3 and Bxh3, shredding my king; 17.dxc6 kept a winning game
4. [middlegame] Answer a wing pawn storm with the central pawn break or file-opening capture, not with a defensive pawn move on the attacked wing Example: Facing ...g5-g4, 17.g3 lost; dxc6 or e5 opened lines toward Black's king first
5. [middlegame] Before any piece move, list every enemy pawn that can capture the destination square; a pawn defender or capturer refutes forks and centralisations Example: ...Qc3 forked rooks but bxc3 took the queen; Ng5 and Nd4 ideas died to hxg5, Bxd4
6. [middlegame] For each candidate, name the opponent's best capture or pawn push reply before choosing; if any wins material or tempo, pick another move Example: ...d5 and ...f5 chased my bishop with no retreat; bxc3 refuted Qc3
7. [middlegame] Each move, list your undefended pieces and squares where one enemy move hits two of them; fix the loosest before starting anything active Example: Loose bishop plus loose pawn handed Black free tempo moves ...Nb4, ...d5, ...f5
8. [middlegame] With a material edge, play the simplest consolidating move and trade pieces (not pawns); refuse clever knight tours that leave pieces loose Example: A piece up, Be4 instead of Nc3 threw away most of the advantage in two moves
9. [middlegame] Keep a knight or bishop on an advanced central square only when a pawn can support it; otherwise it is a target chased by pawns or rooks Example: Bd5 and Be4 were chased by pawns because c4/d3 support never arrived
10. [opening] Before an exchange sequence on an open file, recount the defenders of your central pawn after every trade has been made Example: 8.a4 axb5 axb5 Rxa8 removed e4's guard and lost the pawn to ...Nxe4
11. [middlegame] Cover enemy entry squares (c2, d3, g4, your sixth rank) with a pawn or piece before invaders arrive; never trade queen for rook voluntarily Example: Opponent's rook reached d6/c6 freely; ...Qc3 bxc3 turned a crushing attack into a lost grind
12. [endgame] Keep extra pawns connected and mutually defended, and choose the recapture that keeps the chain linked even if it doubles a pawn Example: 29...dxc4 scattered a6/c4/c6 and Rd6-b6-a6 ate them; bxc4 held everything together
13. [endgame] Never let a lone bishop be the sole defender of your pawns: a rook attacking it forces a losing trade and the whole queenside falls Example: 33.Rd6/34.Rc6 hit the b6 bishop until Bd6 Rxd6 won bishop and every pawn
14. [endgame] Before your king grabs material on the edge, count the pawn race and confirm your king stays inside the square of every enemy passer Example: 44...Kxh4 won a rook but f4-f5-f6 and h-pawn promoted two unstoppable queens
15. [endgame] Before simplifying, name the exact ending you enter and confirm it is a known win; bishop plus two pawns versus rook is usually only a draw Example: Trading into rook-versus-bishop after Qc3 was objectively lost despite "extra pawns"
