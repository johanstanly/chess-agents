# Magnus's notebook: rules from my earlier games

1. [middlegame] Before any queen or piece move, check every enemy bishop, knight and rook line hitting the destination square; a developed bishop covers squares two files away Example: 13...Qc7?? ran into 14.Bxc7: White's Bf4 already guarded c7, 46%→3%
2. [middlegame] Before any quiet or developing move, name your loose pieces and list every enemy check and capture on them; if one wins material, choose another move Example: 11.Bc4?? Qh4+ 12.Bf2 Qxc4 with king uncastled, 55%→8%
3. [middlegame] When a piece is attacked, also weigh pawn moves that counterattack or gain a tempo, and test whether a check first wins your intended retreat square Example: 25...Nd6? lost to 26.Qg5+ Kh7 27.Qxf6 then Qxd6; 25...c5 held
4. [middlegame] Play every capture chain on a square to the end, recounting attackers and defenders after each trade; a piece never defends the square it moves to Example: ...Qc3 "forked" rooks but bxc3 won the queen; 27...Rf5 was "backed" by the rook that had moved
5. [middlegame] Before trading a minor piece, play the recapture: if a pawn recapture opens a file or diagonal toward your king or leaves pawns loose, keep the piece Example: 17...Nc4 18.Bxc4 dxc4 opened the e-file for Re4 and left c4 hanging, 51%→9%
6. [middlegame] Keep a piece on an advanced square only if a pawn can support it; retreat it before enemy pawns take away every escape square Example: Unsupported Ne4 had no retreat after e5/d6; Bd5 and Be4 were chased by pawns
7. [middlegame] Never push pawns in front of your castled king while an enemy bishop or queen eyes those squares; answer a wing storm with a central break instead Example: 17.g3 invited ...fxg3 and ...Bxh3; 17.dxc6 kept a winning game
8. [middlegame] When your rook can invade the enemy second rank hitting loose pawns, prefer that to any equal trade; with exposed kings activity beats simplification Example: Instead of 17...Nc4, 17...Rf2 grabbing h2 and tying White down was winning
9. [endgame] With extra material and rooks on an open file, do not offer the trade to a defended enemy rook; insert a check first and keep both rooks active Example: 34...Re2 allowed 35.Rxe2; 34...Re1+ drove the king back, 98% vs 91%
10. [endgame] With extra material, trade one-for-one but never your last extra piece; cut off his king, take the 7th rank, and push a pawn only with escort Example: Refusing 31.Rd5 dropped 94%→61%; 59...b5 pushed an unescorted pawn instead of Rd5
11. [endgame] Before trading into a pure pawn endgame, count both kings' distance to every pawn and the race in moves; equal material loses if his king is faster Example: 28...Rxe5 29.Rxe5 looked drawable 4v4, but White's central king outpaced mine, 50%→6%
12. [endgame] Keep extra pawns connected, stay inside the square of every passer, and before each move check enemy Kx-captures and stalemate traps Example: 66...Qc3+? Kxc3 drew a won queen endgame; 29...dxc4 scattered a6/c4/c6
13. [opening] Delay the central break (d5/e5) until minors are developed and the king is castled; when two units can recapture, use the minor piece, not the queen Example: 8...d5 exd5 left a backward pawn, 48%→42%; 10...Qxd5? 11.Qxd5 lost a tempo
14. [middlegame] After castling opposite wings, spend a tempo on Kb1/Kh1 and lift a rook (Rh4) along a file fixed by your own wing pawn, instead of trading attackers Example: Caro-Kann 8.h5 h6: 13.Kb1 then Rh4 beat 13.Ne4, which swapped off the attack
15. [middlegame] When a bishop pins your knight against king or a loose rook, kick the pinner with a3/h3 or block with Bd2 rather than defending passively with the queen Example: 12.Qd2 let ...Nc5-e4 pile on; 12.a3 questioned Bb4 at once, 59%→54%
