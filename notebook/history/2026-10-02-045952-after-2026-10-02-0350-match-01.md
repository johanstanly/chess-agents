# Magnus's notebook: rules from my earlier games

1. [middlegame] When your piece is attacked and a pawn near your king is loose, defend with a developing move (Nc3, Nbd2, Be3) instead of shuffling the attacked piece Example: Move 14 Bd5 was hit by Nb4; Be4 "defended d3" but Nc3 was far better and kept +87%
2. [middlegame] Before putting a bishop on a central square, check every enemy pawn that can advance to attack it (d5, f5, e5, c5) and verify a safe retreat exists Example: Be4 met ...d5/...f5; the bishop had no square (Bf5 Qxf5, Bg6 fxg6) and was lost for two pawns
3. [middlegame] Never swap a bishop or knight for two pawns while pieces are active; first search all checks and sacrifices that win the piece back or expose the king Example: Trapped on e4 I played Bxd5 for pawns; Bxh7+ first was stronger and kept more winning chances
4. [middlegame] With a clear material edge, play the simplest consolidating developing move; avoid clever maneuvers that put pieces on squares pawns can attack Example: A piece up at 87%, Be4 instead of Nc3 threw away most of the advantage in two moves
5. [opening] Before any exchange sequence on an open file, recount defenders of your central pawn e4/d4 after the trades Example: 8.a4 axb5 9.axb5 Rxa8 allowed 10...Nxe4 because the rook trade cost e4 its guard
6. [middlegame] When choosing between recaptures, prefer taking the opponent's most active piece over a greedy pawn grab that lets their piece escape with tempo Example: 19.dxc5 instead of Rxe5 let ...Rd8 and ...Nd3 infiltrate; evaluation fell from 54% to 44%
7. [middlegame] Deny enemy knights the d3/c2/f3 outposts: control them with a rook, bishop or pawn (c3, Be3) before the knight arrives, not after Example: Black's ...Nd3 repeatedly harassed my rook and queen because no piece covered d3
8. [middlegame] If two enemy knights attack one pawn defended only by your queen, add a defender with a knight or advance the pawn; a queen-only guard invites Nxd3 removal tactics Example: d3 was hit by Nb4 and Nc5 with just Qd1 guarding, forcing awkward bishop moves
9. [middlegame] Before every move, list your undefended pieces and the squares where one enemy move can hit two of them; fix the loosest one first Example: Bishop on d5/e4 plus pawn d3 both loose gave Black tempo moves ...Nb4, ...d5, ...f5
10. [middlegame] When your bishop pair is deep in enemy territory on light squares, keep a pawn or knight supporting the outpost, else the bishop is just a target Example: Bd5 and Be4 were both chased by pawns because c4/d3 support never came
11. [endgame] With an extra pawn and rooks on, activate the rook behind or beside the passer and trade off the opponent's active knight before advancing Example: After c5 my passer went nowhere while Black's knight on d3/b3 tied my pieces down
12. [endgame] Before simplifying into a pawn-up ending, check the opponent's knight cannot reach a fork square near your rook and king; trade knights if it can Example: Nxc5/Nb3 kept forking ideas alive and nearly equalized a winning position
13. [middlegame] Convert an attack by looking for back-rank and last-rank rook invasions (R to 8th) once the opponent's pieces drift to one wing Example: 24.Rd8 worked only because Black's knight was on b3 and queen on a6, far from the king
14. [opening] In the Spanish with Ba4/Bb3, delay flank pawn pushes (a4) until Nc3 or d3/c3 is in and e4 has two defenders Example: 8.a4 came before Nc3/Nbd2 and the resulting trades cost the e4 pawn
15. [middlegame] Compare candidate moves by asking "what is my opponent's best reply pawn push?" before deciding; if a pawn push gains a tempo on you, pick another move Example: ...d5 and ...f5 both gained tempo on Be4; checking pawn pushes first would have revealed Nc3
