# Magnus's notebook: rules from my earlier games

1. [middlegame] Before any quiet or developing move, list every enemy check; if a check wins a loose piece (Qa5+/Qh4+ hitting a stray knight or bishop), choose another move. Example: 11.Bc4?? Qh4+ 12.Bf2 Qxc4: f3 plus uncastled king made the e1-h4 check decisive, 55%→8%
2. [middlegame] Count all attackers and defenders of the target square and play the whole recapture chain to its end; move only if the final material count favours you. Example: ...Qc3 "forked" rooks but bxc3 won the queen; 26.Ne5 Rxe5 Nxe5 Rxe5 gave two knights for a rook
3. [middlegame] Every move, name your undefended pieces and the entry squares (c2, d3, g4, your 6th/7th rank) an enemy piece can reach; guard the loosest first. Example: Loose bishop plus loose pawn gave Black free tempi ...Nb4, ...d5, ...f5; the rook then invaded d6-c6
4. [opening] When a developed piece is attacked, prefer a pawn block that also defends your centre over retreating the piece to its starting square. Example: 8...Qa5+ : 9.Nc3 undeveloped the knight; 9.c3 held d4 and kept the pawn mass, 57%→53%
5. [middlegame] When a bishop pins your knight against king or loose rook, kick the pinner with a3/h3 (or Bd2) rather than passively defending with the queen. Example: 12.Qd2 let ...Nc5-e4 pile on; 12.a3 questioned Bb4 at once, 59%→54%
6. [middlegame] When your queen is attacked by an enemy piece you can capture, calculate the capture first; losing queen for the attacking pieces is fine when it nets material. Example: 17.Qd3 shuffled; 17.Nxe4 Bxd2 was better since I was already a queen for a rook up
7. [middlegame] Keep a knight or bishop on an advanced central square only if a pawn can support it; retreat before enemy pawns take away every square. Example: Unsupported Ne4 had no safe retreat after e5/d6; Bd5 and Be4 were chased by pawns
8. [middlegame] Never push pawns in front of your castled king while an enemy bishop or queen eyes those squares; meet a wing storm with a central break instead. Example: 17.g3 invited fxg3 and Bxh3; 17.dxc6 kept a winning game
9. [opening] Delay the central break (d5/e5) until your minor pieces are out and your king is castled; opened lines favour the better developed side. Example: 8...d5 exd5 with Nb8 and king home left a backward e5 pawn and open e-file, 48%→42%
10. [opening] When two pieces can recapture, use the minor piece; recapturing with the queen onto a square the enemy queen eyes hands over a free trade and a tempo. Example: 10...Qxd5? 11.Qxd5 Bxd5 reached the same structure a tempo down with queens gone, 45%→38%
11. [opening] Before starting an exchange sequence on an open file, recount the defenders of your central pawns after every single trade, not just the first. Example: 8.a4 axb5 axb5 Rxa8 removed e4's guard and lost the pawn to ...Nxe4
12. [endgame] Never put a piece on a square the enemy king attacks unless you guard it; check Kx-captures and stalemate before every move, even in won positions. Example: 66...Qc3+? Kxc3 drew a won queen endgame; earlier 58...Rd4 allowed 60.Kxd4
13. [endgame] When material ahead, accept every equal trade and simplify; rook plus pawns beats bishop plus pawns, and only bare rook versus bishop is drawn. Example: Refusing 31.Rd5 trade "to keep the exchange" dropped winning chances 94%→61%
14. [endgame] With extra material, first cut off the enemy king and seize the seventh rank; advance a pawn only when king or rook escorts it. Example: 59...b5 pushed an unescorted pawn instead of Rd5; loose pawns let the king counterattack
15. [endgame] Keep extra pawns connected and mutually defended, and before grabbing material check your king stays inside the square of every enemy passer. Example: 29...dxc4 scattered a6/c4/c6; 44...Kxh4 won a rook but f4-f5-f6 plus the h-pawn made two queens
