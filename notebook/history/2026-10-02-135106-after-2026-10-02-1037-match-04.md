# Magnus's notebook: rules from my earlier games

1. [endgame] With queen versus lone king, never put the queen on a square touching that king unless your own king guards it; test Kxq before every check Example: 66...Qc3+? Kxc3 threw away a won K+Q vs K and drew
2. [middlegame] Before any move, list every enemy unit that can capture the destination square — pawn, piece, or king — and only play it if the recapture count favours you Example: ...Qc3 forked rooks but bxc3 took the queen; Ng5 died to hxg5
3. [opening] When an enemy pawn wedges into your camp on the 6th/7th rank, capture or blockade it immediately; piece trades that leave it alive cost a piece later Example: 10...Nxd2 ignored the d6 pawn; cxd6 was needed, d6-d7xe7 wrecked my structure
4. [opening] When your bishop is attacked, choose the retreat that checks or gains a tempo and prevents the opponent's planned central push, not the passive square Example: 6...Bb6 instead of Bb4+ allowed e5, d6 wedge; winning chances fell 50%→32%
5. [middlegame] Keep a knight or bishop on an advanced central square only if a pawn can support it; otherwise retreat it before pawns or rooks chase it Example: Unsupported Ne4 had no safe squares after e5/d6; Bd5 and Be4 were chased by pawns
6. [middlegame] Count the whole capture sequence on a square, not defender numbers: two enemy rooks hitting a knight defended by a knight wins two pieces for a rook Example: 26.Ne5: Rxe5 Nxe5 Rxe5 gave up two knights for one rook
7. [middlegame] If your piece is already hit by stacked heavy pieces, trade it or retreat it at once; adding a cheap defender only feeds the exchange sequence Example: 27.Nhf3 "defended" Ne5; Rxe5 Nxe5 Rxe5 collected both knights instead
8. [middlegame] Never move pawns in front of your king while an enemy bishop or queen eyes those squares; meet a wing pawn storm with a central break or file-opening capture Example: 17.g3 invited fxg3 and Bxh3; 17.dxc6 kept a winning game
9. [middlegame] Each move, name your undefended pieces and squares where one enemy move hits two of them; fix the loosest before starting anything active Example: Loose bishop plus loose pawn gave Black free tempi: ...Nb4, ...d5, ...f5
10. [middlegame] With a material edge play the simplest consolidating move and trade pieces, not pawns; refuse knight tours that leave pieces loose Example: A piece up, Be4 instead of Nc3 threw away most of the advantage
11. [middlegame] Never give queen for rook or less voluntarily, and guard enemy entry squares (c2, d3, g4, your sixth rank) with pawn or piece before invaders arrive Example: ...Qc3 bxc3 turned a crushing attack into a lost grind; rook reached d6-c6 freely
12. [opening] Before an exchange sequence on an open file, recount the defenders of your central pawn after every trade has been made Example: 8.a4 axb5 axb5 Rxa8 removed e4's guard and lost the pawn to ...Nxe4
13. [endgame] Keep extra pawns connected and mutually defended; pick the recapture that keeps the chain linked even if it doubles a pawn Example: 29...dxc4 scattered a6/c4/c6 and Rd6-b6-a6 ate them; bxc4 held everything
14. [endgame] Before your king grabs material, count the pawn race and confirm your king stays inside the square of every enemy passer Example: 44...Kxh4 won a rook but f4-f5-f6 and the h-pawn made two queens
15. [endgame] Before simplifying, name the exact ending you enter and confirm it is a known win; bishop or pawns versus rook is usually only a draw Example: Trading into rook-versus-bishop after Qc3 was objectively lost despite "extra pawns"
