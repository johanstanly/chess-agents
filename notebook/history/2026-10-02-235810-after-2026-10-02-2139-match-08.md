# Magnus's notebook: rules from my earlier games

1. [middlegame] When offering a trade, name the defender of the target square in the position after your move; a piece never defends the square it is moving to. Example: 27...Rf5 "backed by my f8 rook" — that rook was the mover; 28.Rxf5 won a clean rook
2. [middlegame] Play every capture chain on a square to its end, recounting attackers and defenders after each trade; move only if the final material count favours you. Example: ...Qc3 "forked" rooks but bxc3 won the queen; 8.a4 axb5 axb5 Rxa8 removed e4's last guard
3. [middlegame] Before trading a minor piece, play the recapture: if a pawn recapture opens a file or diagonal toward your king, or leaves pawns loose, keep the piece. Example: 17...Nc4 18.Bxc4 dxc4 opened the e-file for Re4 and left c4 hanging, 51%→9%
4. [endgame] Before trading the last rooks into a pure pawn endgame, count both kings' distances to every pawn and the race in moves; equal pawns lose if their king is faster. Example: 28...Rxe5 29.Rxe5 gave a "drawable" 4v4, but White's central king outpaced mine, 50%→6%
5. [middlegame] When a rook can invade the enemy second rank hitting loose pawns, prefer that to any equal trade; with both kings exposed, activity beats simplification. Example: Instead of 17...Nc4, 17...Rf2 taking h2 and tying White down was winning
6. [middlegame] Before any quiet or developing move, list every enemy check; if a check wins a loose piece (Qa5+/Qh4+), choose another move. Example: 11.Bc4?? Qh4+ 12.Bf2 Qxc4: f3 plus uncastled king made the e1-h4 check decisive, 55%→8%
7. [middlegame] Every move, name your undefended pieces and the entry squares (c2, d3, g4, your 6th/7th rank) an enemy piece can reach; guard the loosest first. Example: Loose bishop plus loose pawn gave Black free tempi ...Nb4, ...d5, ...f5; a rook then invaded d6-c6
8. [endgame] When material ahead, trade one-for-one to keep your extra unit, but never trade your last extra piece or a pawn that is your only winning asset. Example: Refusing 31.Rd5 "to keep the exchange" dropped winning chances 94%→61%
9. [endgame] Never place a piece on a square the enemy king attacks unless you guard it; before every move in won positions check Kx-captures and stalemate. Example: 66...Qc3+? Kxc3 drew a won queen endgame; earlier 58...Rd4 allowed 60.Kxd4
10. [endgame] With extra material, first cut off the enemy king and seize the seventh rank; push a pawn only when king or rook escorts it. Example: 59...b5 pushed an unescorted pawn instead of Rd5; loose pawns let the enemy king counterattack
11. [endgame] Keep extra pawns connected and mutually defended; before grabbing material check your king stays inside the square of every enemy passer. Example: 29...dxc4 scattered a6/c4/c6; 44...Kxh4 won a rook but f5-f6 plus the h-pawn made two queens
12. [middlegame] When a bishop pins your knight against king or loose rook, kick the pinner with a3/h3 or Bd2 instead of passively defending with the queen. Example: 12.Qd2 let ...Nc5-e4 pile on; 12.a3 questioned Bb4 at once, 59%→54%
13. [middlegame] Keep a knight or bishop on an advanced square only if a pawn can support it; retreat before enemy pawns take every square away. Example: Unsupported Ne4 had no safe retreat after e5/d6; Bd5 and Be4 were chased by pawns
14. [middlegame] Never push pawns in front of your castled king while an enemy bishop or queen eyes those squares; answer a wing storm with a central break. Example: 17.g3 invited fxg3 and Bxh3; 17.dxc6 kept a winning game
15. [opening] Delay the central break (d5/e5) until minors are developed and the king is castled; when two pieces can recapture, use the minor piece, not the queen. Example: 8...d5 exd5 left a backward pawn, 48%→42%; 10...Qxd5? 11.Qxd5 lost a tempo, 45%→38%
