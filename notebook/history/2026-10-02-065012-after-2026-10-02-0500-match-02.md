# Magnus's notebook: rules from my earlier games

1. [middlegame] Before any queen or piece move, list every enemy PAWN that captures the destination square; a pawn defender refutes any fork or double attack Example: 26...Qc3 forked rooks on a1/d2 but 27.bxc3 simply took the queen for a pawn
2. [middlegame] When ahead, win material by force (hanging piece, clean pin) rather than by a fork; verify the forking square is attacked by nothing at all Example: At 98% winning, Qc3's "double attack" lost the queen; quiet Qe4 kept everything
3. [middlegame] Before every move, list your undefended pieces and squares where one enemy move hits two of them; fix the loosest first Example: Loose bishop plus loose pawn gave the opponent free tempo moves ...Nb4, ...d5, ...f5
4. [middlegame] Ask "what is my opponent's best pawn push/capture reply?" for each candidate; if a pawn gains tempo or wins material, pick another move Example: ...d5 and ...f5 chased a bishop with no retreat; bxc3 refuted Qc3
5. [endgame] With bishop plus pawns versus rook, keep pawns connected and defended by each other or the king; isolated pawns on open lines are eaten one by one Example: 29...dxc4 and 31...c5 left a6/c4/c6 scattered; Rd6-b6-a6 collected them all
6. [endgame] When choosing a recapture with the enemy rook active, pick the one that keeps your pawn chain linked, even if it concedes a doubled pawn Example: 29...bxc4 kept c6+c4+a6 linked; 29...dxc4 broke the mass and dropped from 50% to 9%
7. [endgame] Do not let your lone bishop be the only defender of pawns: a rook attacking the bishop forces trade or loss of the whole queenside Example: 33.Rd6/34.Rc6 hit the b6 bishop until Bd6 Rxd6 won it and every pawn after
8. [endgame] Before your king captures material on the edge, count the pawn race: check your king stays inside the square of all enemy passers Example: 44...Kxh4 won a rook but f4-f5-f6 and h4-h8 promoted two queens unstoppably
9. [middlegame] Never trade queen for rook (or piece for two pawns) voluntarily; keep the queen to escort passers and give checks against a bare king Example: 26...Qc3 27.bxc3 turned a crushing attack into a hopeless bishop-versus-rook grind
10. [middlegame] Deny enemy knights and rooks the d3/c2/f3/6th-rank entry squares with a pawn or piece before they arrive, not after Example: Opponent's rook reached d6/c6 freely once my pawns no longer covered those squares
11. [endgame] With extra pawns, advance them with king support only; an unsupported push that frees a square for the enemy rook loses tempo and the pawn Example: 31...c5 vacated squares and let 32.Rd6 attack bishop and pawns at once
12. [opening] Before an exchange sequence on an open file, recount defenders of your central pawn after the trades Example: 8.a4 axb5 axb5 Rxa8 removed e4's guard and lost the pawn to ...Nxe4
13. [middlegame] Keep a bishop or knight on an advanced square only if a pawn supports it; otherwise it is a target that loses tempo Example: Bd5 and Be4 were chased by pawns because c4/d3 support never came
14. [middlegame] With a clear material edge, play the simplest consolidating developing move and trade pieces, not pawns; avoid clever maneuvers Example: Being a piece up, Be4 instead of Nc3 threw away most of the advantage in two moves
15. [endgame] Before simplifying, name the exact ending you are entering and confirm it is a known win; bishop+2 pawns versus rook is usually only a draw Example: Trading into rook-versus-bishop after Qc3 was objectively lost despite "extra pawns"
