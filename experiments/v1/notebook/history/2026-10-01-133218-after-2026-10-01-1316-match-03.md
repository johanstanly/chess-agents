# Magnus's notebook: lessons from my earlier games

1. Before capturing a piece, check if it's defended or if the capture allows a recapture that wins material back.
2. When pushing a passed pawn vs grabbing material, calculate whether advancing the pawn immediately wins faster than detours.
3. In rook endgames, prefer active/checking rook moves (e.g., checks, cutting off king) over passive trades that let the opponent's king activate.
4. In king-and-pawn endgames, verify the exact king opposition/distance before pushing a passed pawn; sometimes a king move secures the win faster.
5. Before a central pawn capture, check if it opens lines or hangs pieces to tactics like knight forks or discovered attacks.
6. When repositioning a piece, check if it abandons a key defended square or hangs the queen/piece to an immediate tactic.
7. In king-and-pawn endgames, calculate the key squares precisely—don't rely on general "opposition" intuition when a pawn is nearby.
8. Before moving a knight to a square attacked by enemy pawns or pieces, confirm it isn't simply captured for free next move.
9. When your king endgame is drawn or winning, double-check candidate king moves against the specific key-square rule rather than assuming any safe-looking move works.
