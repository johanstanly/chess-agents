# Magnus's notebook: lessons from my earlier games

1. Before capturing or trading, explicitly list every enemy piece (pawns, knights, bishops) attacking the destination/recapture square, not just the obvious one.
2. Before moving/placing a piece on a square, check opponent's immediate pawn pushes or moves that could trap, fork, or win it next move.
3. In king-and-pawn endgames, calculate exact key squares/opposition precisely rather than relying on general intuition.
4. In rook endgames, keep rooks active and opponent's king cut off; avoid trading down to simplify when behind or when activity favors you.
5. When ahead with a passed pawn, calculate whether pushing it immediately wins faster than detours or material grabs.
6. Before simplifying (trades, queen swaps, declining captures), scan for stronger alternatives that win more material or keep initiative alive.
7. When choosing between retreat/repositioning squares, prefer the one safest from enemy pawn advances and not abandoning key defended squares.
8. After opponent offers a trade or capture, check if an alternative response wins material instead of recapturing automatically.
9. Before any capture, verify the piece isn't defended in a way that allows a favorable recapture or tactic (forks, discoveries) against you.
10. Double-check candidate moves against concrete calculation (key squares, attacking pieces) rather than assuming "safe-looking" moves are correct.
